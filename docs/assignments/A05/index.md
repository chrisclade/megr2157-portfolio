# A5 – Bracket Design

## Objective  
  
For this project, I was tasked with designing and analyzing a bracket using normal stress, bending stress, and stiffness equations. The bracket must hold a symmetrical force of 600 lbf and fit over the provided graphic of a Rigid T Beam. A safety factor of 4 must be applied during calculations. The material I chose to design the bracket with was one of three provided options, A36 Steel.

## Calculating Dimensions from Stress Analysis  
  
I start each feature by listing all my known values, unknown values, and assumptions. Then, I sketch a FBD to get a general idea of how to solve for each Feature. After the FBD is completed, I wrote out my known or given equations and used them to solve for my unknown dimensions. I followed this process for all 5 Features.

## Lessons Learned  
  
**Governing failure mode:**  
For Feature A, stress governed the final dimension. The stress analysis required a minimum radius of 0.398 in and the stiffness analysis only required 0.154 in.  
**Error propagation:**  
The radius calculated for Feature A was used in Feature B to find the width. The width was then used to calculate the dimensions for Feature B, which then aided in the calculations of future Features. Drawing a FBD at every step helped visualize the forces to see if the Feature was not in equilibrium, which would have only snowballed into more errors had it not been.  
**Assumption sensitivity:**  
One important assumption made was that shear deflection was negligible. If it was not negligible, the total deflection of the bracket features would be larger and every single one of the stiffness analysis dimensions would need to be adjusted to account for that.
